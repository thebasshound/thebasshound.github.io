Governed AI agents

What an agent is, how much autonomy to give it and what to require before it works on an audit.

Concepts

Automate, assist or act

AUTOMATION

Runs rules

It always does the same thing, like a macro or an analytics script.

ASSISTANT

Answers

It answers what you ask. You decide what to do with the answer.

AGENT

Acts

It receives a goal, plans the steps, uses tools and comes back with evidence.

Anatomy

What you hand to an agent

Treat it like a new auditor: it gets a work order, limited keys and a log it must keep.

GOAL

Work order

What it must achieve and how you know it is done.

TOOLS

Keys

Which systems it can enter. Read-only where possible.

MEMORY

Log

Every step, prompt and output is recorded.

LIMITS

What it may not do

Pay, delete, send or decide without a person.

The data does not travel to the model: the agent works inside the organization's environment and leaves the records where they were.

Autonomy

The higher the impact, the lower the autonomy

Autonomy is set per task. The final report and the conclusion on a control always belong to a person.

1 · SuggestIt proposes ideas; you do the work.
2 · PrepareIt leaves a draft; you review it.
3 · Act with approvalIt acts once a person authorizes.
4 · Act and reportIt acts alone and reports. Low-impact tasks only.

Risks

How an agent fails

RISK

Hidden instructions

A document carries text the agent takes as an order.

RISK

Excessive permissions

It can do more than its task requires.

RISK

Chained errors

A mistake in step one travels all the way to the report.

RISK

No traceability

Nobody can reconstruct what it did or with what data.

Pilot

A controlled pilot in six steps

  1. Pick one specific control and test.
  2. Set the success criterion: the same accuracy as the manual test and at least 50% less time.
  3. Run one cycle in parallel with the manual test.
  4. Review 100% of outputs at first; then exceptions plus a sample.
  5. Version it: document the agent, its instructions and its sources.
  6. Decide with data whether to scale, adjust or retire it.

Metrics to decide: accuracy against the manual test, time per test, population coverage, true versus false exceptions and human correction rate.
