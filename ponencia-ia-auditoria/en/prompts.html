Prompts for auditors

Build 16 audit prompts with your own context, practice with three exercises and take away six habits that turn every prompt into evidence.

Basics

Eight components, one prompt

The IIA's prompting guide for internal auditors identifies eight components. You do not always need all of them: role, context, task and format already change the result.

You are a senior internal auditor specializing in procurement. We are auditing Accounts Payable at a mid-sized manufacturer running SAP; the period has 12,480 invoices. Propose 10 procedures to test the three-way match. Return a table: Risk · Procedure · Test type · Evidence. Preventive controls only; two sentences per cell at most. Use this style: “Inspect that every invoice has an approved PO and receipt before payment.” Before listing, think step by step about where the control can fail. If you are missing information, ask me before answering.
  • Role: Perspective and vocabulary
  • Context: What the AI does not know about you
  • Task: What to do, specifically
  • Format: How to deliver it
  • Constraints: Scope limits
  • Example: The style you expect
  • Reasoning: Think before answering
  • Conversation: Ask if something is missing
Techniques

Five techniques and when to use them

The riskier the task, the more structure and human review it needs.

TechniqueWhenExample
Zero-shotSimple, low-risk tasks“List five fraud risk indicators in payroll.”
Few-shotWhen format matters“Here is a model action plan. Write one like it for…”
Step by stepRisks, walkthroughs and control gaps“Think step by step through vendor onboarding risks, then summarize the top five.”
Role promptingTo take a specialist's view“You are a SOX 404 expert. Would this control meet what the external auditor expects?”
IterativePlanning and complex engagementsRisks → objective for risk 3 → evidence and sampling → workpaper template

Practice

Three guided exercises

Write your version, check it against the list and compare with the solution. What you write is not saved or sent.

EXERCISE 1

Fix this prompt

“Review this control and tell me if it is OK.”

Control: “The Purchasing manager periodically reviews additions and changes to the vendor master.” Rewrite the prompt using at least five of the eight components.

Self-check

Show solution

You are a controls audit specialist. The control belongs to the Accounts Payable process; vendors are created in SAP. Identify (A) design deficiencies, (B) ambiguous wording that makes it hard to test and (C) improvements. Answer in a table with those three columns. Do not assess operation, only design. If you need process data, ask me.

A good answer notes that “periodically” gives no frequency, that it is unclear whether all additions or a sample are reviewed, what evidence is left, whether duties are segregated between who creates and who reviews, and what happens when an error is found.

EXERCISE 3

Draft a finding and have it challenged

Fictitious case: $300,208 was paid to a vendor 14 hours after its bank account was changed, with no evidence of validation. Write the prompt for the draft finding and a second one asking the AI to review it skeptically.

Show solution

The AI drafts and reviews; the Chief Audit Executive signs.

Responsible use

Six habits that make a prompt evidence

  1. Keep the prompt, with its requirements and constraints: it is the audit trail.
  2. Document how you validated the output and against which source.
  3. Separate AI-assisted analysis from your own conclusions.
  4. Make sure you can explain the result in plain language.
  5. Bring AI use into the audit methodology.
  6. Disclose where AI was used when appropriate.
